Week 03 / Digital Methods

Web scraping
reflection.

A small, practical exploration of how a scraper can turn a streaming platform’s homepage into structured data — and what that dataset can and cannot tell us.

TARGET · CHANNEL 4METHOD · WEB SCRAPERCAPTURE · 16 OCT 2026
01 / The question

What does the homepage make visible?

I used Web Scraper to collect programme titles and related text from the Channel 4 homepage. I wanted to see how the page groups programmes and how a visual browsing experience can be represented as rows and fields.

The homepage is not a neutral catalogue. Its collections organise programmes into editorial sections, so the categories themselves are part of what the scrape captures.

02 / The process

From page to dataset

I configured a sitemap in the Web Scraper browser extension and selected collection names, programme titles, descriptive text and image URLs.

CHANNEL4.COM→SELECT ELEMENTS→CSV EXPORT

The exported CSV contains 6 collection records. Each record has repeated fields for programme titles and image links, with descriptive text captured for some items.

04 / What I learned

Structure is useful — and incomplete

The scrape made it easier to compare categories and titles across the page. A structured dataset can help answer questions such as which kinds of collections appear on a homepage, or which titles sit together.

But it records what my selectors could identify at one moment. It does not explain why a programme was promoted, how users interpret the page, or how recommendations differ between people.

05 / Limits & next steps

A snapshot, not the whole platform

  • The export is a snapshot of the homepage on 16 October 2026; page content can change.
  • Repeated fields make the CSV wide, and some text is visibly shortened with ellipses.
  • Selectors may miss content loaded later, hidden below the fold, or presented differently on another device.
  • Image links and titles do not reveal audience behaviour, personalisation, or the editorial decisions behind placement.

Next, I would check the page manually against the export, document the selector choices, and compare repeated captures before making claims about visibility or promotion.
